# System Design Interview Corpus

## Requirements & Capacity Estimation
Clarify scope, users, traffic, storage, bandwidth, latency, availability, consistency, and cost targets.
## APIs & Communication Protocols
Choose REST, RPC, GraphQL, WebSockets, synchronous calls, or asynchronous messages by need.
## Load Balancing
Cover layer 4 and 7 balancing, algorithms, health checks, sticky sessions, failover, and global routing.
## Caching
Design client, CDN, service, and database caches with TTL, eviction, invalidation, and stampede protection.
## Data Modeling
Model entities and access patterns, choose keys and constraints, and justify normalization or denormalization.
## SQL vs NoSQL
Choose relational, document, key-value, wide-column, search, or graph stores based on guarantees and workload.
## Replication
Compare leader-follower, multi-leader, leaderless, synchronous, asynchronous, quorum, and failover approaches.
## Partitioning & Sharding
Select shard keys, distribute data, rebalance, avoid hotspots, and handle cross-shard operations.
## Message Queues & Event Streaming
Use brokers and logs for decoupling, ordering, retries, delivery semantics, replay, and idempotency.
## Consistency & CAP
Reason about consistency models, partitions, availability, quorums, conflicts, and eventual convergence.
## Availability & Fault Tolerance
Use redundancy, timeouts, retries, backoff, circuit breakers, bulkheads, and graceful degradation.
## CDNs & Object Storage
Serve large immutable objects using edge caching, blob storage, signed access, and lifecycle policies.
## Observability
Design logs, metrics, traces, correlation, dashboards, alerts, SLIs, SLOs, and incident evidence.
## System Security
Apply identity, authorization, encryption, rate limiting, secrets, validation, and threat modeling.
## Architecture Trade-offs
Present end-to-end designs, identify bottlenecks and failures, and justify complexity, cost, and evolution choices.
