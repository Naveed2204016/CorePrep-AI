# Object-Oriented Programming Interview Corpus

## OOP Foundations
Objects combine identity, state, and behavior; classes define their structure. Use OOP to model responsibilities, not merely to group functions.
## Classes & Objects
Design cohesive classes with small public APIs and valid invariants. Distinguish instance and class-level state.
## Encapsulation
Hide representation details, control mutation, and expose behavior that keeps objects valid.
## Abstraction & Interfaces
Express stable contracts independently of implementations. Depend on abstractions at change-prone boundaries.
## Inheritance
Inheritance models a true substitutable is-a relationship; deep hierarchies increase coupling and fragility.
## Polymorphism
Dynamic dispatch lets callers use a common contract while concrete types provide different behavior.
## Composition
Composition and delegation model has-a relationships and usually provide more flexible reuse than inheritance.
## Object Construction
Constructors and factories should produce valid objects. Understand initialization, immutability, and lifecycle ownership.
## Method Overloading & Overriding
Overloading is compile-time signature selection; overriding is runtime replacement of inherited behavior.
## SOLID Principles
Use SRP, OCP, LSP, ISP, and DIP as design heuristics and explain their trade-offs with examples.
## Design Patterns
Recognize common creational, structural, and behavioral patterns; apply them only when the problem warrants them.
## Exception Handling
Preserve failure context, define exception boundaries, clean up resources, and avoid swallowing errors.
## Generics & Collections
Generics provide reusable type-safe abstractions. Choose collections by access, ordering, duplication, and mutation needs.
## Equality & Object Contracts
Equality, hashing, ordering, and string representation must remain consistent and predictable.
## Object-Oriented Design
Assign responsibilities, identify collaborations, reduce coupling, and design testable boundaries from requirements.
