# Computer Networks Interview Corpus

## OSI & TCP/IP Models
Map protocols and responsibilities across layers and trace encapsulation from application to link.
## Physical & Data Link Layers
Cover signals, framing, media access, error detection, switching, and reliable link delivery.
## Ethernet, MAC & VLANs
Understand MAC learning, switching, broadcast and collision domains, VLANs, and trunking.
## IP Addressing & Subnetting
Calculate IPv4 and IPv6 prefixes, subnet ranges, masks, CIDR aggregation, and address allocation.
## ARP, ICMP & NAT
Explain address resolution, diagnostic messages, private addressing, translation, and forwarding behavior.
## Routing
Use routing tables and longest-prefix matching; compare distance-vector, link-state, and BGP concepts.
## TCP
Cover handshakes, sequence numbers, acknowledgements, retransmission, flow control, congestion control, and teardown.
## UDP
Understand datagrams, connectionless delivery, loss and ordering trade-offs, and low-latency use cases.
## DNS
Trace recursive and iterative resolution, records, caching, TTLs, delegation, and DNS security.
## HTTP & HTTPS
Cover methods, status codes, headers, cookies, caching, persistent connections, and HTTP versions.
## TLS
Explain certificates, trust chains, handshakes, key exchange, encryption, integrity, and common failures.
## Sockets & Client-Server Communication
Use addresses, ports, sockets, connection lifecycles, multiplexing, and request-response communication.
## Firewalls & Network Security
Apply filtering, segmentation, proxies, VPNs, IDS/IPS, least privilege, and zero-trust ideas.
## Wireless Networks
Cover Wi-Fi standards, association, channels, interference, roaming, and wireless authentication.
## Network Troubleshooting
Use layered diagnosis with ping, traceroute, DNS lookup, port tests, routing inspection, and packet capture.
